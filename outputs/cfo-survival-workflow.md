# The Solo Consultant CFO Survival Workflow

**Built for:** A one-person consulting business (advisory contracts + cohort training programs, occasional subcontractors, zero full-time employees).

**Time budget:** Under 2 hours/week total — 5 minutes daily, 90 minutes every Friday, plus one 30-minute monthly close.

**Purpose:** Stop guessing. Know your runway, your breakeven, your true margins, and your next 13 weeks of cash — every week, without hiring a bookkeeper or learning GAAP.

---

## 0. The Two Numbers Everything Else Is Built On

Before the workflow, calculate these two formulas once, then update them monthly. Everything below refers back to them.

### Formula A — Minimum Viable Retainer Run-Rate (MVRR)
The monthly recurring revenue you need just to stop bleeding reserves.

```
MVRR = (Fixed Monthly Overhead + Your Minimum Personal Draw + Debt Service) 
       ÷ (1 − Variable Cost %)
```

- **Fixed Monthly Overhead** = software, insurance, office/coworking, subcontractor retainers, accounting fees — anything you pay whether or not you close a deal.
- **Minimum Personal Draw** = the number you need to pay yourself to not touch savings (not your ideal salary — your survival number).
- **Variable Cost %** = payment processing fees, referral commissions, subcontractor delivery costs as a % of revenue.

**Example:** Overhead $3,500 + Draw $6,000 + Debt $500 = $10,000. Variable costs run 8% of revenue.
MVRR = $10,000 ÷ 0.92 = **$10,870/month you must generate, collected, every month.**

This is your breakeven line. Anything below it and you're funding the business personally.

### Formula B — True Effective Hourly Rate (TEHR)
The real rate you're earning per fixed-fee advisory engagement, once you count everything.

```
TEHR = Total Fee Collected ÷ (Delivery Hours + Admin Hours + Sales/Proposal Hours + Rework Hours)
```

Track four hour buckets per project, not one. Most solo consultants only count delivery hours and think they're charging $250/hr when the real number — once you add the 6 hours spent on the proposal, 4 hours of scope-creep rework, and 3 hours of invoicing/admin — is $140/hr.

**Rule:** If TEHR on a project falls below 1.5x your minimum viable hourly rate (MVRR ÷ realistic monthly billable hours), that engagement type is a **pricing problem**, not a volume problem. Raise the fee or cut the scope before you take another one like it.

## 1. The Daily 5-Minute Vital Sign Check

Do this before opening email or Slack. Three numbers, one spreadsheet tab or one dashboard view. No analysis — just read and note if any is in the red zone.

| # | Metric | How to Calculate | Red Zone Trigger |
|---|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Cash Runway in Days** | Current bank balance ÷ Average daily cash burn (trailing 30-day average of outflows minus inflows) | Under 60 days |
| 2 | **Aging Accounts Receivable** | Sum of unpaid invoices, bucketed 0–30 / 31–60 / 61+ days overdue | Any invoice crosses into 31–60 without a follow-up sent |
| 3 | **Today's Revenue Realization** | (Amount actually collected today) vs. (Amount you billed/should have billed today based on project delivery) | Collected < 70% of billed for 3+ consecutive days |

**Setup (one-time, 20 minutes):** A single Google Sheet with three cells pulling from your bank export (manual or via Automation section below) and your invoicing tool. Color-code with conditional formatting: green/yellow/red. You are reading a traffic light, not doing accounting.

**Rule:** If Cash Runway drops below 60 days, that day's first task — before any client work — is either (a) an invoicing push on aging AR, or (b) a pipeline push on proposals sitting in "sent" stage. Not both. Pick the one with the faster cash-in-hand path.

## 2. The Weekly Solo Financial Routine (Friday Mornings, 90 Minutes)

Block it on your calendar like a client meeting. No exceptions, no rescheduling into "when things calm down."

### Block 1 (25 min): Earned vs. Invoiced Reconciliation

For long advisory engagements paid in milestones or monthly installments, "cash collected" and "revenue actually earned" drift apart — and that drift is what makes you feel richer or poorer than you are.

**Method:**
1. For each active advisory project, calculate **% Complete** using the simplest honest proxy: hours delivered ÷ total scoped hours, OR milestones completed ÷ total milestones — pick one method per project and stay consistent.
2. **Earned Revenue** = Total Contract Value × % Complete.
3. **Invoiced Revenue** = What you've actually billed to date.
4. **Gap = Earned − Invoiced.**
   - Gap is **positive** (earned more than invoiced) → you're financing the client. Invoice now.
   - Gap is **negative** (invoiced more than earned) → you're ahead of delivery. Fine, but don't let it exceed 20% of contract value or you're at risk of a clawback conversation if the project ends early.

Keep this on one row per project in your tracker. Five projects = 5 minutes of math.

### Block 2 (25 min): Scope Creep Audit

For every active advisory project:

```
Scope Variance % = (Actual Hours Logged to Date − Budgeted Hours to Date) ÷ Budgeted Hours to Date
```

- **Budgeted Hours to Date** = total scoped hours × % of project timeline elapsed (not % complete — timeline elapsed, so you catch drift early).
- **Trigger points:**
  - Variance > 15% → send a scope-check email to the client this week, referencing the original SOW, before it grows.
  - Variance > 30% → this is a change-order conversation, not a "we'll absorb it" situation. Draft the change order this Friday, send Monday.

This single check is what prevents the "I'm slammed but broke" trap — you catch margin erosion at 15%, not at project close when it's too late to charge for it.

### Block 3 (20 min): Retainer Renewal Risk Check

For every retainer/recurring engagement, score three signals, updated weekly:

| Signal | Green | Yellow | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Responsiveness | Replies within 48h | Replies within a week | Ghosting >1 week |
| Engagement usage | Using full scope of retainer | Using <50% of scope | Near-zero usage last 30 days |
| Renewal conversation | Already confirmed next term | Not yet discussed, <45 days to renewal | <30 days to renewal, no conversation started |

**Any retainer with two or more yellow/red signals goes on this week's "renewal risk" list.** Your action this week: send one relationship-check or value-recap touchpoint to every retainer on that list — not a renewal pitch, a value reminder (a mini-results summary, a relevant insight). Pitch the renewal itself 30 days out, minimum.

**Output:** A running list — Retainer Name | Monthly Value | Renewal Date | Risk Score | This Week's Action. This list feeds directly into your 13-week forecast (Section 4).

### Block 4 (20 min): Expense & Draw Check

- **Categorize your top 3 variable expenses** (typically: subcontractors, software/tools, travel-for-delivery) and cap each at a fixed % of trailing-90-day gross revenue — e.g., subcontractors ≤ 20%, software ≤ 5%, travel ≤ 8% of revenue. If any category is trending over cap this week, that's this Friday's cut decision, not next quarter's.
- Confirm your **owner's draw** for the week/month is at or below the Minimum Personal Draw from Formula A — never at "whatever's left in the account."

## 3. The Monthly "Solo Board of Directors" Package (4 Slides)

Present this to yourself, your spouse, or your accountant on the first Monday of each month. Takes 30 minutes to build if your weekly tracker is current. This is a board meeting where you are the entire board — treat it with the same discipline as if a real investor were reading it.

### Slide 1: Cash & Runway
- Line chart: bank balance, trailing 13 weeks, with your MVRR line overlaid.
- Cash Runway in Days (from Daily Check), trend vs. last month (arrow up/down).
- One sentence: "At current burn, we run out of cash on [date]" — or "Runway is growing, current trajectory adds X days/month."

### Slide 2: Revenue Quality
- Bar chart: Earned Revenue vs. Invoiced Revenue vs. Cash Collected, by month, last 6 months.
- Revenue mix pie: Advisory vs. Training, this month vs. trailing 3-month average (shows if you're becoming dangerously concentrated in one stream).
- Aging AR total and % over 60 days.

### Slide 3: Project & Engagement Profitability
- Table: each active/closed engagement this month, Contract Value, TEHR (Formula B), Scope Variance %.
- Flag (red text) any engagement with TEHR below 1.5x your target hourly rate.
- One line: "This month's pricing problem" or "This month's scope problem" — name the specific project.

### Slide 4: Breakeven Analysis (the one that answers "am I actually surviving")

This is the slide that tells you the exact monthly revenue needed to survive. Build it like this:

**Step 1 — Fixed Costs (monthly):**
List everything you pay regardless of revenue: rent/coworking, software subscriptions, insurance, loan payments, retained subcontractor fees, your Minimum Personal Draw. Sum = **Total Fixed Costs**.

**Step 2 — Contribution Margin %:**
```
Contribution Margin % = (Revenue − Variable Costs) ÷ Revenue
```
Variable costs = anything that scales with delivery: per-project subcontractors, payment processing, delivery materials/venue costs for training cohorts, travel tied to specific engagements. Calculate this from trailing 3-month actuals.

**Step 3 — Breakeven Revenue:**
```
Breakeven Revenue = Total Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin %
```

**Example:** Fixed Costs $10,000/month. Contribution Margin 78% (you keep $0.78 of every revenue dollar after variable delivery costs). Breakeven = $10,000 ÷ 0.78 = **$12,820/month.**

**Chart it:** A simple bar showing Breakeven Revenue vs. Actual Revenue, last 6 months, with a horizontal breakeven line. This single visual answers Challenge #4 (pricing vs. volume problem) directly:

- If actual revenue is consistently near or above breakeven but reserves are still eroding → you're pricing correctly but **your Contribution Margin is too thin** (delivery/variable costs too high relative to fees) — a pricing problem.
- If actual revenue is consistently below breakeven → **you don't have enough deals/cohorts closing** — a volume/pipeline problem, not a rate problem.
- If both revenue is below breakeven AND TEHR (Slide 3) is below target → you have both problems simultaneously, and volume should be fixed first (a pricing increase on too little pipeline won't save you).

## 4. The 13-Week Forecasting Engine

A rolling cash forecast, rebuilt every Friday during your weekly routine (add 15 minutes to Block 3, or do it as a monthly deep-dive — either works, but weekly catches problems faster).

### Step 1: Build the base — known cash in/out
For each of the next 13 weeks, list:
- **Confirmed inflows:** signed contract milestones due, confirmed training cohort payments, active retainer invoices.
- **Confirmed outflows:** fixed overhead (spread across weeks it's actually paid), subcontractor payments, loan payments, your draw.

This gives you a "floor" cash position — what happens if you close zero new business for 13 weeks.

### Step 2: Weight the advisory pipeline by stage
Apply a probability multiplier to every open opportunity, and only add the **weighted value** to the week you realistically expect to invoice/collect it:

| Pipeline Stage | Probability Weight |
|---|---|
| Discovery call held | 10% |
| Proposal sent | 50% |
| Verbal commitment received | 90% |
| Contract signed (moves to "confirmed," Step 1) | 100% |

```
Weighted Pipeline Value (per deal) = Deal Value × Stage Probability
```

Place the weighted value in the week you expect the first invoice/payment to land — not the week you expect to close the deal (there's usually a lag between close and cash).

### Step 3: Weight retainer renewals
Use your Retainer Renewal Risk score from the weekly routine (Section 2, Block 3) to assign probability:

| Risk Score | Renewal Probability |
|---|---|
| All green | 90% |
| One yellow | 65% |
| Two+ yellow/red | 30% |
| Any red on responsiveness or usage | 15% |

Apply this weight to the retainer's monthly value in the weeks it would renew/invoice within the 13-week window. Retainers currently active and not yet at a renewal decision point stay at 100% (they're confirmed until their renewal date).

### Step 4: Combine into three scenarios
For each of the 13 weeks, produce:
- **Floor (Confirmed only):** Step 1 alone. Your worst realistic case.
- **Weighted (Base Case):** Step 1 + weighted pipeline (Step 2) + weighted renewals (Step 3). This is the number you actually plan against.
- **Best Case:** Step 1 + pipeline at 100% (everything closes) — useful only to sanity-check upside, never plan cash against it.

### Step 5: Read it every Friday
- Plot Weighted Base Case cash balance across the 13 weeks. If it dips below **4 weeks of Total Fixed Costs (Formula A)** at any point in the forecast, that's your trigger to act now — not when you get there: intensify pipeline activity, follow up on aging AR, or delay a discretionary expense.
- If the Floor scenario alone keeps you above breakeven for 13 weeks, you're in a genuinely safe position regardless of what closes.
- Track forecast accuracy monthly: compare last month's Week-4 forecast to actual. If you're consistently over-forecasting (pipeline weights too optimistic), tighten your probability weights — e.g., drop "proposal sent" to 35% if your actual close rate at that stage runs below 50%.

## 5. Automation for One Person

Goal: kill manual data entry for expenses and time-tracking, and make the weekly routine mostly a **review**, not a **build**.

### The Stack (lightweight, low/no-code)
1. **Scheduling → CRM:** Calendly (or similar) triggers a new record in your CRM (Notion, HubSpot Free, or Airtable) automatically when a discovery call is booked — via native integration or **Zapier/Make**. This auto-creates the pipeline entry at "Discovery call held" stage (10% weight) with zero manual typing.
2. **CRM → Pipeline stage automation:** Set up a Zapier/Make automation: when you move a deal card to "Proposal Sent" in your CRM, it auto-updates the stage field that feeds your 13-week forecast weighting (Section 4, Step 2). If you're not ready for automation tooling, a simple CRM view filtered/sorted by stage does the same job manually in under 2 minutes on Friday.
3. **Time tracking → Invoicing:** Use **Toggl Track or Harvest** (both have generous free/cheap tiers) tagged by project. Both integrate natively with **Xero, FreshBooks, and QuickBooks** — tracked hours flow straight into invoices, eliminating the "reconstruct my week from memory" problem and giving you the raw hours for TEHR (Formula B) and Scope Variance (Section 2, Block 2) automatically.
4. **Invoicing → Bank/AR tracking:** Xero or FreshBooks bank feed sync gives you real-time Aging AR (Daily Check #2) without manual reconciliation — connect it once, check it daily.
5. **Expenses:** Connect a dedicated business card/account directly to Xero/FreshBooks bank feed with auto-categorization rules set once (e.g., "any charge from [software vendor] = Software expense category"). This removes 90% of manual expense entry — you're only touching the 10% that don't auto-categorize.
6. **Weekly dashboard pull:** If using Airtable/Notion as your CRM, build one dashboard view that pulls: AR aging (via Xero/FreshBooks API or manual CSV import weekly), pipeline weighted value (from CRM stages), and retainer risk scores (a simple 3-field manual update, since relationship judgment can't be automated). This becomes the single tab you open every Friday for Section 2.

**Reality check for a solo operator:** Don't build all six at once. Sequence: (1) time-tracking → invoicing first (highest time savings, kills the worst manual task), (2) bank feed → AR next (removes daily manual checking), (3) Calendly → CRM last (nice-to-have, smallest time cost since it's just deal creation). Each one is a 15–30 minute one-time setup. Total automation build time: under 2 hours, one time, in exchange for reclaiming ~90 minutes/week permanently.

## Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Every morning | Cash Runway, Aging AR, Today's Revenue Realization | 5 min |
| Every Friday | Earned vs. Invoiced, Scope Creep Audit, Renewal Risk, Expense/Draw Check, 13-Week Forecast update | 90 min |
| First Monday of month | 4-slide Solo Board Package (Cash/Runway, Revenue Quality, Project Profitability, Breakeven) | 30 min |
| Once, then monthly refresh | Recalculate MVRR and TEHR | 15 min |

**The one rule that matters most:** every red-zone trigger above has a same-week action attached to it. A metric you check but don't act on is just anxiety with extra steps. This workflow only works if the red zones translate into the first task of your next working day.
